Security here means what it means in crypto: testing and code security. What follows is what the public site states at [figaroprotocol.com/security](https://figaroprotocol.com/security).

## External audit

The security work is the project's own, at every layer of the stack; the audit scope and the handover are kept current for an external audit, whose results will be published when they exist — findings, remediations, and the auditor's report, not a summary of them.

## Verifying what you are served

The contracts being verified says nothing about the page in front of you — a frontend could still misrepresent what you sign. The definitive, non-technical answer to that is a verifiable build: a release pinned by its own content hash (a CID) with a published rebuild recipe anyone can diff against it, plus a second, independently built frontend attesting to the same typed data — so no single origin's word is required at all. Until then, the two checks under [signing, on the FAQ](https://figaroprotocol.com/faq#signing) are the check, and the limitation stated there stands as written.

## The verification stack

What is in place today is a verification stack — seven independent benches: six on the protocol's contracts and one on the equilibrium algebra those contracts enforce — each from a different angle. Seven, and not one, because each catches a class of defect the others structurally cannot. What each method covers and what it structurally cannot reach are set out bench by bench below, and the bounds the two bounded benches — TLA+ and Halmos — run under follow the list; what each class of check reaches and cannot, and the scope of the claim the checks support, is argued in [What Formal Verification Transfers to a Bonded Contract](https://figaroprotocol.com/papers/verified-resolution-kernel):

- **Foundry**: the unit + integration suite — 421 test functions. Class: *behavioral and regression* — what a contract does on concrete inputs, and what it stops doing after an edit. It proves nothing about the inputs nobody wrote a test for.
- **Halmos**: 41 symbolic-execution properties, proved exhaustively. Class: *the untried input* — a property is proved over every input in range at once rather than sampled, so an arithmetic or boundary case no test writer imagined cannot hide.
- **Certora**: 50 formal rules across eight CVL specs — `FigaroCore`, its own token operations, attestation, batch token operations, the batch state root's chain, the florin token, `RpgfMinter`, and the registry `RpgfMinter` reads for each kind. Class: *state-transition* — rules quantified over reachable states and arbitrary calls, so a violation returns as a concrete counterexample trace, not a hunch.
- **TLA+**: model-checking of five models in seven configurations — four protocol state machines on-chain (`FigaroCore`, the florin token, one machine holding the two resolution paths, and the swap/commit coordinator) and, beside them, the off-chain relay's state machine, checked in three configurations. Class: *temporal and liveness* — ordering, interleaving and races across a whole protocol, and states that can be entered but never left. No per-function bench can see these.
- **Echidna**: 15 property-based fuzzing targets on `FigaroCore` and the florin token. Class: *adversarial sequence* — randomized call sequences hunting for an ORDER of operations that breaks an invariant, which is how an attacker actually looks.
- **Lean 4**: the bonded chain's equilibrium — the one derived in [Asymmetric Bonding and Buyer Dominance](https://figaroprotocol.com/papers/asymmetric-bonding) — proved over the exact payoff table the TLA+ invariants pin to the shipped `FigaroCore` — resolving beats withholding after performance, performing beats holding out at every retention a seller could manage, and no unilateral deviation profits at any position of a chain of arbitrary length, with the cumulative accumulator derived rather than assumed. Class: *the choosing* — what a rational party does given the numbers, which no model checker can express; dependency-free, so an auditor rebuilds it cold.
- **Static analysis**: Slither's 100 detectors and Semgrep's smart-contract ruleset, over the audit scope, on every push. Class: *the cataloged defect* — vulnerability patterns already known and named, matched in the source without running it, so a reentrant call, an unchecked return value, or a comparison on a block timestamp is flagged before any test runs. It cannot reach anything that needs the mechanism's meaning — whether the bond arithmetic is right, or who may resolve. Every result it returns is triaged one by one in [docs/AUDITOR_HANDOVER.md](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro/blob/main/docs/AUDITOR_HANDOVER.md).

### The bounds

A bounded check holds inside its bounds and says nothing past them. Each bound below is read from the file that sets it in the [public repository](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro), so a re-run reproduces it.

**TLA+** — `scripts/test-tla.sh` runs TLC (`-workers auto`) on each configuration in `formal/`; it reads `formal/tla2tools.jar`, which you download, or the path in `TLA2TOOLS`. Every configuration sets `CHECK_DEADLOCK FALSE`: a run ends where its constants are exhausted, and that end state is the bound, not a violation.

- `FigaroCore` — `formal/MC.cfg`, constants bound in `formal/MC.tla`: two buyers and two sellers, an initial balance of 30 each, payments 1–3, at most two processes, at most two sub-orders per process (a chain of up to three orders). Nine invariants.
- The florin token — `formal/FlorinToken.cfg`: a maximum supply of 5, three minters (`deployer`, `minter1`, `minter2`), and six recipients, the zero address among them. Eight invariants.
- The two resolution paths — `formal/ResolutionUniverses.cfg`: one buyer, two sellers, two artifacts, an initial balance of 12, payments 1–2, at most two processes with one sub-order each, two accrual periods, a seller floor of 1 (the floor disabled, so one counted process scores; re-running with 2 exercises the floor), and both named assumptions, `AssumeDomainSeparation` and `AssumeAccrualGatesAligned`, `TRUE`, as shipped (flipping either to `FALSE` is the experiment, and is expected to fail). Twenty-one invariants.
- The swap/commit coordinator — `formal/WitnessSwapAndCommitCoordinator.cfg`: payments 1–2, an expected cumulative value up to 1 above the payment, a Permit2 maximum input of 1–3, a swap output of 0–4, a party bond of 6, a party input of 3, router liquidity of 12, and three sequential calls. Ten invariants.
- The off-chain relay — one module, `formal/RelayState.tla`, that models the relay's batch-path state and is checked in three configurations, each with every design switch it carries set `TRUE`, as the relay must run (flipping one to `FALSE` is the experiment, and is expected to fail):
  - its state lifecycle under crashes, failed writes, refused batches, resends and takeover — `formal/RelayState.cfg`: two relays, two parties, at most three batches. Eight invariants and two temporal properties.
  - the deferral of an operation dropped at batch formation — `formal/RelayStateDeferral.cfg`: one relay, one party, one batch, five operations (fixed in the module), a drain cap of 3, a deferral window of 2 ticks, a run of 4 ticks, a re-queue cap of 1 and an attempt bound of 1. Ten invariants.
  - the buyer's resolve authorization — `formal/RelayStateResolve.cfg`: one relay, one party, one batch, three orders and payments 1–2 (both fixed in the module). Three invariants.

**Halmos** — `scripts/test-halmos.sh` runs halmos (CI pins `halmos==0.3.3`) with Z3, in seven passes over the four harness files in `test/`. It passes no `--loop` or `--depth`, so halmos's defaults hold: a loop whose condition is symbolic is unrolled at most twice on a path (loops with a concrete condition run in full), path depth and path count are unlimited, and halmos prints a warning naming the loop bound when a path was cut by it. The per-assertion solver timeout is 600,000 ms, overridden by `HALMOS_SOLVER_TIMEOUT_MS`. The input ranges are the harnesses' `vm.assume` lines: in `test/core/kernel/HalmosFigaroCore.t.sol` a single order's payment runs from 1 to `type(uint128).max / 4` with twice the payment within a starting balance of `type(uint128).max`, and the two-order chain's payments each run from 1 to `type(uint64).max`; in `test/build/rewards/HalmosUsageCounter.t.sol` a timestamp runs to 10,000 past the fixture's second period; in `test/app/HalmosMembersRegistry.t.sol` a stake and a cooldown are nonzero and the elapsed time falls inside the cooldown; the clause, agreement and assembly registry harnesses constrain only keys (nonzero, distinct).

Verification is a precondition for external audit. It is not a substitute for one. The protocol's position is that an audit should examine a surface that has already been pushed against from this many directions. The current contract inventory is [Contracts](/protocol/contracts/); the property-by-property map — each protocol property beside the Solidity that enforces it, the tests that regression-check it, and the formal layer that proves it — is the [Verification map](/verification/verification-map/); the full harness inventory — commands, layers, and what each bench owns — is [docs/TESTING.md](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro/blob/main/docs/TESTING.md) in the repository.

## Nothing to pause or upgrade

FigaroCore has no admin, no pause function, no upgrade key, no governance with discretionary power over tokens. The Core does not contain code that any address can call to halt resolution, blacklist a participant, or move tokens it does not have a signed commitment against. There is nothing to capture because there is no privileged role to hold. (As stated on [the FAQ](https://figaroprotocol.com/faq#shutdown).)

## Reporting a vulnerability

Found a vulnerability? Report it privately — [GitHub private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro/security/advisories/new), or the alternate channel in [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro/blob/main/SECURITY.md) — never a public issue.
