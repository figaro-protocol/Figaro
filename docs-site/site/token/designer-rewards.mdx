600M florins are reserved for the designers whose clauses, registered agreements and assemblies the network actually uses. The reward is retroactive and it is uniform: a designer earns for real recorded usage alone, and nothing about who they are, what category their work falls in, or how the DAO feels about it enters the meter. Neutrality is achieved by the stake, not by weighting.

Read the [whole model](/) first, and [The contracts](/protocol/contracts/) for the two surfaces named below (`UsageCounter`, `RpgfMinter`). The reasoning this page states in brief is owned in full by [`docs/DESIGNER_REWARDS.md`](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro/blob/main/docs/DESIGNER_REWARDS.md); the florin itself and the reserve's place in the supply are on [Florin](/token/florin/); the discretionary, human-judged half is the [DAO](/token/dao/) treasury, at a different tier.

## Who earns

A **designer of record** is the wallet that wrote and registered a clause, a agreement or an assembly — and it holds that standing for exactly as long as its registration stake in that entry's own registry stays live. Clauses, registered agreements and assemblies earn on the same terms, from the same pool, through the same call, with no seniority between them, and the reward keeps the three families apart: every count, score and claim is keyed by `(kind, key)` — kind `0` a clause key, kind `1` an assembly `assemblyHash`, kind `2` a registered agreement's `agreementHash` — and each kind is checked against its own registry only (`src/build/rewards/UsageCounter.sol:165-171`). A registry takes any bytes32 but its reserved keys, so a wallet can register an existing assembly's `assemblyHash` as a clause key or an agreement hash; that registration earns nothing of the assembly's reward.

Two consequences people expect to be otherwise:

- **The mandatory clauses earn.** `figaro-commerce` and `figaro-topology` ride on every order and are scored for their designer of record like any other clause. "Mandatory" is a registration-layer convention about what a runtime composes; nothing on chain privileges or exempts them. A registered agreement that carries them earns for its own designer of record under kind `2`, beside them: nothing is excluded.
- **Registering is the whole of the claim.** First-write-wins: whoever registers a clause key under their wallet is its designer of record, and the binding is irreversible. Competing with a better clause under your own wallet is equally permissionless.

## The meter is uniform

A clause's, registered agreement's or assembly's score in a period is its real usage alone:

```
score = icbrt(c · d² · 1e18)
```

where `c` is the count of distinct resolved processes that used it and `d` the number of distinct live-staked sellers who carried it. Breadth is weighted above raw volume (the score is proportional to `c^(1/3) · d^(2/3)`), and below a minimum-support floor of distinct staked sellers the score is zero — counting continues, and the full score springs when the floor is met. Value is deliberately not a term: the protocol's cost to move one unit equals its cost to move a trillion.

**There is no tag, category, weight, per-wallet cap, match round, or quadratic component, and none may be added.** A per-clause multiplier would say some kinds of contribution are worth more than their use — a judgment the mechanism exists to avoid. That judgment lives at a different tier, in the DAO treasury.

`icbrt(c · d² · 1e18)` is the formula in `UsageCounter._score` (`src/build/rewards/UsageCounter.sol:804`), and the on-chain `icbrt` (`:820`) mirrors the SDK's so an off-chain recompute agrees exactly.

## Neutrality comes from the stake

What keeps the meter honest against a wallet that manufactures usage is not a cap on what it can earn but a **two-sided live ETH stake** any manufactured trade must carry, in the base chain's own currency, at the moment it counts:

- **Seller side.** Usage counts only for a seller of record whose `MembersRegistry` stake is live. `UsageCounter` gates every use on `MembersRegistry.registered(seller)`, on either route: a direct call reverts and a batch skips that one use (`src/build/rewards/UsageCounter.sol:756`, `:706`). A seller enters a period's breadth once, whichever route carried their trade.
- **Designer side.** A claim rewards only entries whose registry stake is live and registered by the claimant. `RpgfMinter._isAuthor(kind, key, account)` verifies each entry against the one registry its kind names and never another — `ClauseRegistry.depositOf` for kind `0`, `AssemblyRegistry.bindings` for kind `1`, `AgreementRegistry.bindings` for kind `2` — requiring `registeredBy == caller` and the stake un-withdrawn (`src/build/rewards/RpgfMinter.sol:297`).

This is a **value loop, not a cost.** The stake is reclaimable, and holding it is exposure to the growth of the network your own work produces. A designer who withdraws de-surfaces the entry for new compositions and stops earning on it, while every order already committed against it still resolves.

## Counting happens when the trade resolves

`UsageCounter` counts usage at resolution, permissionlessly, from facts the chain already holds: the order is resolved (`orderStatus == 2`) and the clause — or the provenance leaf naming the registered agreement and the assembly — was merkle-committed in the signed `checkoutHash`, the same leaf shape the [attestation coordinator](/protocol/contracts/) checks, with the status gate inverted. Anyone may call `recordClauseUsage` (`UsageCounter.sol:461`, counted under kind `0`), `recordAssemblyUsage` (`:542`, kind `1`) or `recordAgreementUsage` (`:504`, kind `2`); the proof is what is trusted, never the caller. A use in a proved batch — its resolution and its inclusion proved by the batch's own proof — reaches the counter through `applyBatchAccrual` (`:661`, verifier-only), and is counted into the same tally, by the same internal path, as a direct call's: a use is a use whichever way its process reached the chain.

The timing is forced, not chosen: the chain cannot look backwards — the Core never calls the registries, and no contract can read an event. So the fact is counted when it occurs; a use left uncounted waits on its seller's stake, and can never count once the last period has closed. Read `scoreOf(kind, clauseOrAssembly, period)` (`:438`), the one tally's score. Only the clause fingerprint reaches calldata, so a private clause's plaintext never becomes public in the counting.

Every committed clause key is scored, and a registered agreement and an assembly are each scored through their own leg. Both designers are credited from the agreement's **provenance leaf** — a reserved leaf of the agreement format, not a clause, whose key is `provenanceLeaf()` = `keccak256("figaro-agreement-provenance")` (`UsageCounter.sol:192`) and whose committed bytes are `{"agreementHash":"0x<64 lowercase hex>","assemblyHash":"0x<64 lowercase hex>"}`. `recordAgreementUsage(order, agreementHash, assemblyHash, proof)` rebuilds that leaf from the two keys and proves it against `checkoutHash`; `recordAssemblyUsage(order, assemblyHash, agreementHash, slotContentHash, leafProof, membershipProof)` proves the same leaf and then the agreement's leaf under `assemblyHash` — the membership proof, from `buildAgreementInclusionProof(assembly, i)`. `buildProvenanceInclusionProof(agreement)` returns the leaf's proof, and `buildUsageClaims` assembles all three kinds' claims. The two reserved keys — the provenance key and the assembly terms key, `keccak256("figaro-assembly-terms")` — earn nothing whatever a registry holds at them: the direct path reverts (`ReservedKeyIsNotARegistration`) and the batch path skips both. The leaf and how a buyer attests it are on [Clauses and agreements](/protocol/clauses/).

## How a designer claims

`RpgfMinter` mints from the 600M reserve, pro rata to real recorded usage, one claim per wallet per closed period:

```solidity
struct ClauseOrAssemblyKey { uint8 kind; bytes32 key; }                                    // RpgfMinter.sol:117
function claim(uint8 periodId, ClauseOrAssemblyKey[] calldata clausesOrAssemblies) external;  // RpgfMinter.sol:219
```

```ts
import { USAGE_KIND_CLAUSE, USAGE_KIND_ASSEMBLY, USAGE_KIND_AGREEMENT,
         RPGF_MINTER_ABI } from "@figaro-protocol/sdk";

const mine = [
  { kind: USAGE_KIND_CLAUSE, key: myClauseKey },          // 0 — checked against ClauseRegistry
  { kind: USAGE_KIND_ASSEMBLY, key: myAssemblyHash },  // 1 — checked against AssemblyRegistry
  { kind: USAGE_KIND_AGREEMENT, key: myAgreementHash },    // 2 — checked against AgreementRegistry
];
await walletClient.writeContract({
  address: addresses.rpgfMinter!, abi: RPGF_MINTER_ABI,
  functionName: "claim", args: [periodId, mine],
});
```

`claim` mints `periodAmount[periodId] · yourScore / totalScoreInPeriod` and requires the period closed — so a share is score-over-total against numbers that stopped moving, with no snapshot and no history walk. Pass every clause, agreement and assembly you designed in one call, as `(kind, key)` pairs; each is verified against the registry its kind names with a live stake, and a repeated `(kind, key)` reverts `DuplicateClauseOrAssembly`. `claimable(periodId, account, pairs)` reads the figure first, and `computeRpgfAllocations` from the SDK returns each wallet's list as `allocation.claims`. This is the designer-side half of the two-sided live-stake gate; the seller-side half is `UsageCounter`'s.

## The schedule, and what does not exist

Accrual buckets into **nine annual periods**. A period's counts are final once it ends, each period's budget rewards that period alone, and claims never expire — a closed period's arithmetic is stable forever, so a consumer reads a number that cannot move. Budgets rise across three groups (15% of the reserve over years 1–2, 30% over years 3–5, 55% over years 6–9, split equally within each group), because the largest share should fall on the most-measured evidence; funding a thin early network is the DAO treasury's job. The pool is fixed: a wallet that manufactures usage only dilutes a period's pool, never inflates it.

Deliberately absent, each absence load-bearing: no posting, bonding, challenge, or forum; no match pool, crowd round, or quadratic funding; no per-clause multiplier, tag, category, or weight; no per-wallet cap and no claim expiry; no admin, pause, sweep, or upgrade; and no emission tied to resolution — florins are minted only through per-period claims. The revert reasons an integrator hits on these surfaces are cataloged on [The contracts](/protocol/contracts/).
