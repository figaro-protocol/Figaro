Figaro is a whole economic system built from one primitive: a bonded commitment that makes cooperation between strangers the equilibrium, with no arbitrator, no timeout, and no admin. This site is the technical manual for building on it. It assumes you have a wallet, `cast`/`viem`, and the [public repository](https://github.com/figaro-protocol/Figaro).

Read the whole model first — the pages below assume it.

## The two mechanisms

Everything rests on two mechanisms that compose:

- **Asymmetric bonding at commit.** The buyer locks twice the payment; each seller locks twice the cumulative value through its link. Each bond is that party's own deterrent, so after performance, cooperating is every party's best move.
- **Buyer dominance with atomic resolution.** Only the buyer can resolve, and resolution pays every seller and refunds every bond at once, or not at all. That is what binds many sellers into one chain that resolves from a single signature.

Neither is tunable: the `2×` ratio and one-buyer-resolves are constitutive, not parameters.

## The nouns

The protocol's nouns are few, and the [lexicon](/start-here/lexicon/) is their one list; the ones a builder meets first:

- **clause** — a term in an agreement; its spec is registered under the hash of its bytes.
- **agreement** — the clauses composed for one trade; its design is registered under its merkle root, and at checkout it is completed into the tree whose root, the `checkoutHash`, both parties sign.
- **assembly** — agreements composed into something anyone can reuse, registered under its root.
- **process** — the runtime instance of an assembly: opened by a buyer's first commitment, extended by each further commitment, ended by the buyer's resolution.
- **commitment** — the object a buyer and a seller both sign, one per seller: the parties, the token, the payment, the running total, the `checkoutHash`, a salt and a deadline.
- **attestation** — a party's signed statement, during the process, about a clause of its agreement; the chain keeps its hash and checks who signed and what it is bound to, never whether it is true.
- **resolution** — the buyer's one act that ends a process: every payment transfers and every bond is refunded in one transaction.
- **composition** — how anything here is built: clauses into agreements, agreements into assemblies, assemblies into processes; a process with other contracts on-chain; a process with legal and regulatory norms off-chain.

Roles are per order, not nouns: in any order the buyer pays and resolves and a seller adds value, and the same wallet can be buyer in one order and seller in the next.

## The lifecycle, and the two paths

An order has two operations and no others: **commit** — both parties' signatures lock their bonds — and the buyer's **resolve** — pay and refund, atomically. A process is a chain of committed orders that resolves as one.

Both operations happen one of two ways, and the two share no state: the **direct path**, each operation a call to `FigaroCore`; and the **batch path**, where a validity proof applies many operations — commits and resolves alike — in one transaction against `FigaroBatchVerifier`. The direct path is always open; the batch path is a scaling convenience.
